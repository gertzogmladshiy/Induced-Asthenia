# Induced-Asthenia
A physics-based, run-heavy football playbook engineered to systematically degrade defensive structural integrity through kinetic attrition and sustained mechanical torque.

The Induced Asthenia Architecture
​Engineered by Dook Ramotar Jr.


​Welcome to the Induced Asthenia Architecture—a football offensive system designed not just to outscore an opponent, but to induce a clinical state of physical and systemic failure in the opposing defense.

​Built for High School, JUCO, and secondary collegiate programs, this playbook abandons the modern passing-spread arms race. Instead, it weaponizes cross-disciplinary physics, leveraging principles from mechanical engineering and the Beer-Lambert Law to turn a football game into a 40-minute war of kinetic attrition.

​By utilizing smaller, faster offensive linemen (sub-5.0 speed, superior leverage) and a relentless, high-possession rushing attack, the system forces larger defensive fronts to absorb maximum physical torque snap after snap. The ultimate objective is mathematical certainty: sustained directional pressure inevitably causes heavy defensive personnel to structurally break down by the fourth quarter.

​Core Systemic Features:
​Kinetic Attrition: Extreme time of possession (60-70%) designed to exhaust D1-sized defensive linemen and short-circuit fast-paced opposing spread offenses.

​The Vector-Bone Base: A highly condensed, heavy-torque alignment built for instant A/B gap pressure and downhill velocity.

​The Lambert Package: Spread-option concepts that utilize the Beer-Lambert shift to increase the "path length" of defensive pursuit, draining lateral stamina.
​Calculated Personnel Specs: Strict physical parameters for the offensive engine, requiring sub-5.0 speed linemen and a 4.2-4.4 speed operational quarterback.

Play: Vector-Bone Right Veer
​Action: The offensive line fires off the ball with low-pad leverage to seal the inside gaps. The QB reads the playside defensive end. If the end crashes down on the FB dive, the QB pulls and vectors outside with the HB as the pitch man.
​Result: High-magnitude collision at the point of attack, bleeding defensive stamina.

Play: The Beer-Lambert Shift to Sweep
​Action: Pre-snap, the Wingback (WB) motions across the formation (The Beer-Lambert Shift), changing the leverage angle. The sub-5.0 playside Guard pulls to the edge.
​Result: The defense has to rapidly shift their run fits. The Guard and TE seal the edge, and the HB hits the C-gap with maximum velocity.

Play: Attenuation Option / Halfback Pass (Trick Play)
​Action: Used when the opposing safeties start creeping down to stop our heavy run game. We fake the sweep, let the defense bite, and throw over their heads. Q tosses wide right to the HB, who fakes the run and throws deep to the WR or TE.
​Result: Because the defense is physically exhausted from the Vector-Bone battering ram, their lateral pursuit is heavily attenuated, and their secondary will overcommit to the run.
